## Snippets (paper-ready prose)

**Purpose:** Carefully worded sentences/paragraphs for later drafting. Prefer these over improvised strong language.  
**Rules:** Quote recipes from protocols/papers. Isolation capital-I = operational product. No slogans.

### R1-S1 — Dual media as practice, not proof of mechanism

> Across institutional protocols and Isolation publications, culture systems commonly use a richer growth medium and a lower-serum (or less enriched) maintenance medium at or near inoculation, then read CPE as evidence of virus. That pattern describes **how Isolation is done**; it does not by itself establish that CPE under those conditions is virus-specific.

### R1-S2 — Capital-I Isolation

> By **Isolation** we mean virology’s operational product of culture, cytopathic readout, and passage (an **Isolate**)—not chemical purity of a particle preparation.

### R1-S3 — The two-tier argument structure (2I1D)

R1 makes **two arguments in sequence**, and the corpus is organized to supply each one:

> The primary argument of R1 is that virology has the **"two independent variables, one dependent variable" (2I1D) pattern** represented throughout the field — not just in virus isolation. But the *first* argument is narrower: virology has **codified** this pattern for the core process of detecting the presence of, and "Isolating", the main-character particle (i.e. "the virus") in its institutional protocols globally, **and** that practitioners in the field generally follow this 2I1D pattern for the **detection and Isolation endpoints**.
>
> The *second* argument is that this 2I1D pattern **persists through the field**, regardless of the endpoint.

In short: **(Arg 1)** the 2I1D (growth→maintenance serum switch, read as CPE) is codified and followed for the *detection/Isolation* endpoint; **(Arg 2)** the same 2I1D pattern recurs across the field even when the *endpoint is something else* (titration, antigen detection, neutralization, plaque enumeration, serology, etc.).

## Corpus organization: category (folder) × class (endpoint)

Two independent axes organize the R1 reference collections. Do **not** conflate them.

### Axis 1 — `category`: what kind of reference is it? (encoded by FOLDER)

The **folder** a reference sits in is the category. The split is fundamental:

| Folder | Category | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `refs/isolation_practice/` | **category 1** | A publication **reporting what was done in practice** (a primary virus-culture/Isolation paper). This is the evidence base for Arg 1 + Arg 2. |
| `refs/isolation_protocols/` | **category 2** | A **protocol / SOP / institutional guideline** prescribing practice (not a report of a practice run). |
| `refs/methods_review_teaching/` | **category 3** | A **methods-review / teaching chapter / thesis** — describes how to culture, or reviews method, without being a field practice report. |

Category 2 and 3 references are **never** stored in `refs/isolation_practice/`; they live in their own folder **with their own CSV**. A reference moved out of `isolation_practice` has its `VI###` prefix stripped and gets the folder's own ID prefix, and its row moves to that folder's CSV.

### Axis 2 — `class`: what is the ENDPOINT? (encoded by CSV column, category-1 only)

Within the **category-1** set (the "reporting practice" papers in `refs/isolation_practice/`), the `class` column differentiates by the **primary endpoint** of the culture work:

| class | Primary endpoint | Arg |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **CPE read as virus detection and/or Isolate** (CPE = "we found / Isolated the virus"). May carry additional secondary endpoints — all are recorded. | Arg 1 — detection/Isolation core |
| **4** | **2I1D serum pattern present, but a different primary endpoint** (titration/TCID₅₀, antigen detection, neutralization, plaque, hemagglutination/hemadsorption, molecular, purification, characterization). | Arg 2 — the pattern persists across the field |

**`class` exists only for category-1 rows.** Category-2 (protocol) and category-3 (methods-review) rows have no `class` — their folder already classifies them.

**Key rule (owner, 2026-11-10):** a category-1 paper is **class 1** iff CPE-as-detection/Isolation is its *primary* (or one of its main) endpoints; otherwise it is **class 4**. **Multi-endpoint papers record ALL endpoints** (every `endpoint_*` column present = yes), not only the primary one; a multi-endpoint paper with CPE-detection among its endpoints is class 1.

### CSV columns (category-1 `isolation-refs.csv`)

- `endpoint_CPE_detection_isolate` — yes/no: is "CPE = virus detection and/or Isolate" a stated endpoint?
- `endpoint_*` — one yes/no column per other endpoint (`endpoint_titration_TCID50`, `endpoint_antigen_detection`, `endpoint_neutralization`, `endpoint_plaque`, `endpoint_hemagglutination_hemadsorption`, `endpoint_molecular`, `endpoint_purification`, `endpoint_characterization`). For multi-endpoint papers, **all** present endpoints are yes.
- `primary_endpoint` — the dominant endpoint (decides class).
- `class` — `1` or `4` (category-1 only).
- **Enrichment columns (`*_pre/post_inoculation`):** `FBS_` (serum), plus `TPB_` (tryptose phosphate broth), `lactalbumin_` (lactalbumin hydrolysate), `yeast_extract_`, `peptone_` — each with a pre and post. See `## Enrichment` below.

## Enrichment (the medium "I" variable): serum vs non-serum supplements

R1's independent variable is **enrichment** — how rich the growth medium is vs the maintenance
medium. Serum is the headline case, but the growth→maintenance switch often changes *more than
one* enrichment at once, and that is itself evidence for the 2I1D argument.

### Counts as serum (`FBS_pre/post_inoculation`)

Whole **serum** of any origin: FBS, FCS, calf, newborn calf, adult ("bovine") serum, γ-irradiated
or heat-inactivated FBS, agammaglobulinemic calf serum. These are undefined whole-serum enrichments
and are the `FBS_` value. If the serum is NOT fetal, note the type in `notes` but still record the
number in the FBS column.

### NOT serum — excluded from the FBS column, but classified as either "stabilizer" or "enrichment"

1. **Stabilizers / single purified molecules** (never a growth-enrichment signal; record in
   `base_medium`/`notes` only): **albumin (BSA/Fraction V)**, **gelatin**, **detergents/Tween/PEG/
   Pluronic**. Also **trypsin** is an enzyme (µg/ml), not a % supplement.
2. **Assay reagents** (never growth enrichment): **blocking/diluent sera** in immunostaining
   (goat/horse/donkey serum named with "block"/"wash"/"antibody").
3. **Non-serum *enrichments*** (NOT serum, so NOT in the `FBS_` column, but **DO** capture their
   pre/post in their own columns): **tryptose phosphate broth (TPB)**, **lactalbumin hydrolysate
   (LAH)**, **yeast extract**, **peptone/proteose peptone**, and (P1-era) **ultrafiltrate**.

**Why the third group matters:** a recipe "10% FBS + 10% TPB → 2% FBS" drops *two* enrichments at
inoculation, not one. Recording TPB as enrichment (in `TPB_pre/post`), and NOT as serum, keeps the
serum statistic clean *and* preserves the richer (multi-enrichment) form of the confound.

**Examples in-corpus:** VI46 (10% TPB growth → dropped), VI219 (0.3% TPB both; +0.02% yeast extract
post), VI222 (10% TPB → dropped), VI229 (10% TPB → 10% TPB + 0.5% LAH), VI235 (10% TPB → dropped),
VI243 (0.5% LAH → 0.5% LAH), VI111 (TPB 2% post).
