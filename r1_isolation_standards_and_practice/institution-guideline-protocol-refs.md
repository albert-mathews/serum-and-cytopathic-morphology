# Description
Collection of institutionbal guildelines with virus isolation/culturing protocols.
The intent here is to capture the protocol steps where innoculum is added, to see what other changes are are simultaneously made to the culture system.
Secondly, the end-point for viral presence is recorded. i.e. is presence of CPE stated as synonymous with viral presence.




# ATCC Virology Culture Guide
https://www.atcc.org/resources/culture-guides/virology-culture-guide

## insitution description
American Type Culture Collection
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATCC_(company)

## Inncoluation
- “Viral growth medium is usually supplemented with a lower percentage of serum than cell growth medium, often ranging between 2-10% depending on the virus” (p. 4)
- “NOTE 5: For viral inoculation, ATCC recommends decreasing the percentage of serum to 2% as it can interfere with viral attachment. This can vary from strain to strain, and is not applicable for influenza viruses.”
- Procedure: Remove cell growth medium, wash monolayer with PBS, inoculate with viral stock prepared in base medium (without serum) unless specified, then add/maintain in viral growth/maintenance medium.

## end points
see section “Viral authentication and viability testing”
- CPE (primary visual indicator; cells checked daily for rounding, refractility, degeneration, etc.)
- Hemagglutination, hemadsorption, sequencing, ELISA (supplemental).

## Discussion
the ATCC is not explicit about the protocol, it is hidden within the 'Notes'.

## Overview
FBS pre-innoculation: 2-10%
FBS post-innoculation: 2%
Is CPE a viral presence indicator: yes




# CLSI M41a
https://clsi.org/shop/standards/m41/

## insitution description
Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_and_Laboratory_Standards_Institute

## Innoculation
- section “5.3.1.2 Cell Density”: “Once the cultures have reached the required density, maintenance medium containing 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS) can be used in place of growth medium, which usually contains 10% FBS. Medium with the lower concentration of FBS will maintain cell viability and will slow monolayer overgrowth.”
- section “5.3.2 Maintenance”: “Refeed monolayers that are at 75% or greater confluence with fresh maintenance medium containing a low concentration of FBS (e.g., 2%) when the medium color indicates that the pH is below 7.0 (yellow-orange to yellow).”
- section “5.4.1 Medium Composition”: “Typically 10% FBS is used for growth medium intended for the propagation of cell culture monolayers, while a concentration of 1 to 3% is utilized to maintain subconfluent to confluent monolayers both before and after inoculation.”
- section “7.1.2 Preinoculation Assessment of Monolayers”: “Inspect cell culture microscopically and macroscopically as described in Section 5.3.1 just prior to specimen inoculation. In summary: the cell culture medium must not be cloudy or turbid; cells should be adherent and healthy in appearance; and the monolayer should be 75 to 90% confluent, but not overgrown.”
## end points
see section “7.4.2 Viral Effects”
- CPE (primary)
- hemagglutination (supplemental)

## Discussion
CLSI describes culture medium with 'cell manuafacturer recommend FBS concentration of FBS'(typically 10%) as 'growth medium'.
CLSI describes culture medium with 2% FBS concentration as 'maintenance medium'.
CLSI dictates that beyond 75% confluence, 'maintenance medium' shall be used.
CLSI dictates that confluencey should be 75-90% for innoculation.

Together, those 4 requirements above mean that in general, pre-innoculation FBS concentration would be typically 10%, while post-innoculation would be 2%.
The protocol does not explicitly state that FBS reduction is coincident with inoculation. Combining the listed constraints is consistent with a typical pattern of ~10% pre-inoculation and ~2% post-inoculation; actual lab practice may vary.

## Overview
FBS pre-innoculation: 10%
FBS post-innoculation: 2%
Is CPE a viral presence indicator: yes

# Rationale / justification for the serum drop at inoculation — verbatim audit (2026-11-14)

**Purpose.** For each institutional protocol reference, record *why* (if at all) the protocol says to
lower serum at or near inoculation, using the verbatim sentence that carries the reason. Where no reason
is stated, record that explicitly ("none stated") — a null finding is itself a result. Quotes are read
from the local PDFs in `refs/isolation_protocols/`; page/section references point into those files.

**Taxonomy of stated rationales (from the text):**
- **attachment** — serum interferes with viral attachment;
- **overgrowth / viability** — lower serum slows monolayer overgrowth and/or maintains cell viability (and so keeps cells in the state required for a readable culture/inoculation);
- **inhibitors** — serum carries viral inhibitors that interfere with infection;
- **none stated** — the step is prescribed without a reason;
- **not a drop** — the protocol does not lower serum at inoculation (hold, omit, or raise).

Summary:

| Protocol (PR#) | Serum change | Stated rationale |
|---|---|---|
| ATCC Virology Culture Guide (PR002) | ~10% → 2% (2–10%) | **attachment** |
| CLSI M41-A (PR003) | ~10% → 2% (1–3%) | **overgrowth/viability + inhibitors + viral-replication** |
| ASM CPE protocols (PR004) | growth → 2% | **none stated** |
| NEADL/CIRAD PPRV SOP (PR005) | 10% → 2% | **none stated** |
| WOAH Terrestrial Manual ch. 3.5.2, 2021 (PR006) | (growth unstated) → 2% FCS | **none stated** |
| WHO EPI measles/rubella manual (PR008) | 10% → 2% | **overgrowth** (+ "overgrown → unsuccessful") |

## Verbatim quotations

### ATCC Virology Culture Guide (PR002) — rationale: attachment
- p.13, SERUM section, NOTE 5: “For viral inoculation, ATCC recommends decreasing the percentage of serum to 2% as it can interfere with viral attachment. This can vary from strain to strain, and is not applicable for influenza viruses.”
- p.4 (step 1, “Initiating Frozen Cultures — Tissue Culture-Adapted Strains”), for the lower-serum recipe without a stated reason: “Viral growth medium is usually supplemented with a lower percentage of serum than cell growth medium, often ranging between 2-10% depending on the virus (See NOTE 1).”
- p.4, NOTE 1 (influenza exception): “Viral growth medium prepared for tissue culture-adapted strains of Influenza should not be supplemented with Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS).”
- Evidence: `[VERIFIED src]` `refs/isolation_protocols/ATCC Virology Guide.pdf` (pp. 4, 13).

### CLSI M41-A (PR003) — rationale: overgrowth/viability + viral-replication + inhibitors
- §5.3.1.2 Cell Density (PDF p. 17): “Once the cultures have reached the required density, maintenance medium containing 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS) can be used in place of growth medium, which usually contains 10% FBS. Medium with the lower concentration of FBS will maintain cell viability and will slow monolayer overgrowth. Monolayers that have become overgrown (see Section 5.1.4) should not be used for viral culture.”
- §5.4.1 Medium Composition (PDF p. 20), the direct function statement: “The lower serum concentration supports cell viability while permitting virus replication.”
- §5.4.1 Medium Composition (PDF p. 20), the inhibitor reason stated generally: “Serum may also contain specific and nonspecific viral inhibitors and their presence may vary by lot.”
- §7.2.3 Culture Inoculation (PDF p. 41), the inhibitor reason stated for influenza: “Because serum may contain viral inhibitors that interfere with infection of cell cultures by influenza viruses, it has been recommended that the monolayers be washed with a serum-free medium just prior to inoculation.”
- §5.3.2 Maintenance (PDF p. 18), the inverse (higher serum = growth): “Growth of sparse monolayers may be enhanced by refeeding with medium containing a higher concentration of FBS (e.g., 10%) and/or by incubation in a CO2 atmosphere.”
- Evidence: `[VERIFIED src]` `refs/isolation_protocols/CLSI M41.M41AE_copypaste.pdf` (pp. 17, 18, 20, 41).
