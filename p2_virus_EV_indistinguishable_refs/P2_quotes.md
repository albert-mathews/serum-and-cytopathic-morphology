## A. Dilemma / co-purification / indistinguishability

### P2-071 Tang et al. 2017 *Sci Rep* (OA) — admits biochemical indistinguishability
> "Retroviruses and small EVs overlap in size, buoyant densities, refractive indices and share many cell-derived surface markers making them virtually indistinguishable by standard biochemical methods. This poses a significant challenge when purifying retroviruses for downstream analyses … given that EVs are a major contaminant of retroviral preparations."

### P2-014 Coren et al. 2008 *Retrovirology* (OA)
> "A major source of this contamination is from vesicles, either microvesicles or exosomes, that have similar physical properties as virions. Thus, these particles can not be removed by size or density fractionation."

### P2-073 Pérez / Ostrowski et al. 2019 *J Extracell Vesicles* (OA) — weakens CD45 / AChE / iodixanol success
> "Similarity of structure and biophysical properties of small EVs and HIV (similar diameters of 50–150 nm, similar densities by flotation in sucrose) makes their separation difficult, and it is now clearly recognized that they are co purified by standard virus or exosomal purification protocols…"

> "…in the T lymphoid cell line Jurkat, CD45 is mainly released in large EVs, and hardly present in small EVs. Thus, the utility of CD45 to separate small EVs and/or exosomes from HIV is limited…"

> "…endogenous EVs are only slightly 'lighter' than the virus, and are recovered in overlapping fractions of such [iodixanol velocity] gradient."

> "…acetylcholinesterase (AChE), which was previously proposed to identify EVs specifically, is in fact mainly present in small non-EV structures…"

### P2-009 Gould et al. 2003 *PNAS* (OA) — Trojan continuum
> "We propose that retroviruses exploit a cell-encoded pathway of intercellular vesicle traffic, exosome exchange, for both the biogenesis of retroviral particles and a low-efficiency but mechanistically important mode of infection."

### P2-001 Nolte-’t Hoen et al. 2016 *PNAS* (local seed PDF)
> Mission-almost-impossible separation language; size/density overlap (see prior extract in useful CSV).

### P2-010 Bess et al. 1997 *Virology* (full text — see §G)

### P2-010 Bess et al. 1997 *Virology* 230:134–144 — **weakens** sucrose/UC purity

**Abstract (p.134):**
> "Microvesicles band in sucrose gradients in a range of densities that includes the same density as retroviruses."

**Discussion (p.143):**
> "We have been unsuccessful in separating microvesicles from HIV-1 by centrifugation techniques (data not shown). However, the lack of gp120SU on microvesicles suggests that protocols employing immunoaffinity techniques may be useful in this separation."

**Also (p.141 region / Results framing):**
> "…proteins bound to nonviral particles (i.e., microvesicles) can copurify with virus…" / microvesicles "which copurify with HIV-1 during sucrose density centrifugation."

**Polarity:** Classic primary **weaken** for density-only claims; opens door to immunoaffinity remediation (later Esser→Trubey→Coren→Ott).

### P2-011 Gluschankof et al. 1997 *Virology* 230:125–133 — **weakens** gradient enrichment

**Abstract (p.125):**
> "…gradient enrichment resulted in the isolation of large quantities of HLA-DR-containing material which banded at a density overlapping that of infectious HIV."

> "Electron microscopy of gradient-enriched preparations from supernatants of virus-infected cells revealed an excess of vesicles with a size range of about 50–500 nm, as opposed to a minor population of virus particles of about 100 nm."

**Results (≈p.129):**
> "HIV particles are a minority population, comprising about 25 to 50% of the vesicles in both infected [preparations]…"

**Discussion (≈p.131):**
> "…vesicles of cellular origin are a major contaminant. … it is unclear why these cellular vesicles … should have the same density as HIV-1 virus particles."

**Also (intro):** contamination with "an excess of nonviral membrane vesicles of cellular origin." Links to Raposo exosomes (shared HLA-DR / size features) without equating identity.

**Polarity:** Parallel 1997 **weaken**; virus often minority of particles in "purified" prep.

### P2-013 Cantin et al. 2008 *J Immunol Methods* 338:21–30 — **strengthen** velocity/AChE (with marker caveats)

**Abstract (p.21):**
> "Retroviruses are therefore likely to contaminate in vitro preparations of exosomes and vice versa and sucrose gradients are inefficient at separating them. However, we have found that their sedimentation velocities in an iodixanol (Optiprep™) velocity gradient are sufficiently different to allow separation and purification of both vesicles."

> "…exosomes collecting at 8.4–12% iodixanol and HIV-1 at 15.6%. We also show that immunodepletion with an anti-acetylcholinesterase antibody rapidly produces highly purified preparations of HIV-1 or exosomes."

**Results:**
> "These results confirm the efficiency of the Optiprep™ velocity gradient method in separating quasi-similar vesicles such as exosomes and HIV-1."

> "About 95% of the AChE activity was recovered with the anti-AChE-beads after the first round of precipitation. … It is thus possible to separate exosomes from HIV-1 virions by specific immunodepletion."

**Discussion (p.28):**
> "In fact, the densities of both exosomes and HIV on sucrose gradient are very similar (1.13 to 1.21 g/mL ml for exosomes and 1.16 to 1.18 g/mL ml for HIV-1)."

> "This is why flow cytometry, ELISA or bead capture techniques based on specific markers are not sufficiently discriminating for the separation of exosomes, MPs and HIV-1."

> "…only AChE was a specific marker of exosomes. AChE appears to be excluded from HIV-1…"

> Notes CD45 (Ott/Trubey) "allows separation of MPs derived from leucocyte plasma membranes only and does not eliminate exosomes originating from the endosomal membrane." Recovery of total load on Optiprep "was not greater than 40%."

**Polarity:** Strong **counterexample** to sucrose-impossibility; still admits density overlap + marker inadequacy; AChE later weakened by Pérez/Ostrowski 2019.

### P2-015 Ott 2009 *Methods Mol Biol* 485:15–25 — **strengthen** remediation protocols

**Abstract (p.15):**
> "…experiments examining retroviruses can be complicated by the contamination of even highly purified virion preparations with nonviral particles (either microvesicles or exosomes)."

> Subtilisin "enzymatically removes the proteins outside the virions"; CD45 immunoaffinity "removes vesicles by capture with antibody-linked microbeads."

**Intro (p.16):**
> "While these nonviral particles have a wide range of densities and sizes, a fraction of them have the same size and density as retroviruses (2, 3). Thus, these vesicular contaminants are impossible to remove by physical methods (4)."

> "This process removes >95% of the proteins associated with microvesicles (7–9)…"

> "CD45 is incorporated into many vesicles released from hematopoeitic cells that co-purify with density-purified virion [preparations]."

> "Thus, removing particles with CD45 removes most of the contamination from density-purified virion preparations."

**Notes (p.21):**
> "Unlike the digestion procedure, CD45-depleted virions maintain their infectivity (4)…"

> "A drawback of this method is the possibility that not all of the vesicles might contain CD45 and remain in the depleted virus preparation."

**Polarity:** Institutionalizes problem + offers non-size/density fixes; subtilisin is exterior shave (not ontology separation); CD45 incomplete + hematopoietic-restricted.

### P2-078 Szilágyi & Cunningham 1991 *J Gen Virol* 72:661–668 — **partial** (H vs L; ≠ host EV)

**Abstract (p.661):**
> "During gradient purification of herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) two bands of particles were observed: a sharp lower band and a more diffuse upper band. The lower band contained almost exclusively HSV-1 virions (H particles) whereas the upper band consisted of membrane-enclosed particles (L particles). These L particles resembled the virions in appearance, but lacked the viral nucleocapsid and were not infectious."

> "This clearly indicates that the novel L particles were not merely virions which had formed without the inclusion of a nucleocapsid or virions which had subsequently lost their nucleocapsid during preparative handling."

**Discussion:**
> "If L particles can be completely separated from infectious virions they could be used as a potential [vaccine]…"

**Polarity:** Density **can** separate virion-related particle subtypes (H vs L). **Hedge:** L-particles are virus-encoded products, not host EVs — interpretive bridge only (cf. Heilingloh 2017).

### P2-080 Reiter et al. 2019 *J Chromatogr A* 1588:77–84 — **strengthen** heparin (VLP vs EV; caveats)

**Abstract (p.77):**
> "Separation of enveloped virus-like particles from other extracellular vesicles is a challenging separation problem due to the similarity of these bionanoparticles."

> "In heparin affinity chromatography 54% of the total particle load were found in the flow-through, and 15% of the particles were eluted during the salt linear gradient. … extracellular vesicles dominate the flow-through fraction, while HIV-1 gag VLPs eluted during the salt gradient."

**Intro/methods framing:**
> "Typical purification methods such as density centrifugation or size exclusion chromatography … are not recommended for efficient separation of EVs and VLPs."

> "A clear discrimination and separation between these bionanoparticles is hard to achieve, as both particles contain proteins from the lipid bilayer of the host cell…"

**Conclusion (≈p.83):**
> "…multimodal flow-through and a heparin binding chromatography is able to separate the enveloped VLPs from EVs. … This is in contradiction to other protocols where the extracellular vesicles are recovered by binding to heparin affinity chromatography."

> "Considering both, particle size distribution and proteomic analysis, we conclude that in P1 HIV-1 gag VLPs are enriched and in FT a heterogeneous mixture of host cell EVs is present."

**Polarity:** Affinity **counterexample** (VLPs bind heparin; EVs mostly FT in this protocol). System is **gag VLPs** (not infectious HIV); enrichment not absolute purity; opposite of Balaj-style EV-binds-heparin protocols — method-conditional.

## H. P2-044 Chen et al. 2015 *Cell* — PS vesicles package enterovirus clusters

**PDF:** `P2-044_chen_2015_PS_vesicles_enterovirus.pdf` (verified Chen et al.; DOI `10.1016/j.cell.2015.01.032`)

> "Clusters of viruses are packaged and released non-lytically in PS lipid vesicles"

> "We show that multiple infectious enteroviral particles are clustered within individual phosphatidylserine (PS) lipid-enriched vesicles and non-lytically secreted out of cells."

> "Per 200 nm cross-section, a single vesicle contained on average 19 ± 3 PV particles (n = 6 vesicles)."

> "These data are consistent with our dSTORM findings (Figures 5A–5D) and transmission electron micrographs of isolated PS vesicles (Figure 5E) and indicate that vesicles contain multiple PV particles, which enable multiple viral RNA genomes to be transferred en bloc into a cell."

> "PS vesicles non-lytically released from cells were selectively enriched in mature enteroviral particles"

> "In summary, we report here a novel mode of viral transmission among cells where multiple viral particles are clustered and collectively released within PS-lipid-enriched vesicles."

**Polarity / bounds:** Full-text primary evidence that enterovirus particles can be packaged in PS vesicles, released without lysis, and transmitted en bloc; this weakens a naked-virion-only framing. It is an enterovirus/poliovirus system and does not by itself establish a universal EV/virion identity or purification rule.
