# R5 — Pre-cell-culture bulk virus purification census (research stream — historical census)

**Folder:** `r5_precell_virus_purification_census/`
**Role:** A historical census answering one question with per-item evidence, not a narrative guess:

> Before cell culture (operationally, before Enders–Weller–Robbins 1949 and the mid-1950s spread of routine
> monolayer methods), **how many viruses were isolated in bulk and physically characterized**, and **by what
> methods**? Which of those were plant viruses, bacteriophages, and animal viruses — and was any animal virus
> crystallized before cell culture?

**Why this stream exists.** The paper's Introduction (and the Discussion-history thread) asserts a scoped,
factual claim: bulk virus purification/crystallization was a *plant-virus-and-phage* capability until cell
culture removed the live-host bottleneck for animal viruses (the first crystallized animal virus, poliovirus,
came only in 1955 and required tissue culture). That claim must be **enumerated and dated per item**, not
repeated as an impression. This stream is where the enumeration lives.

**Status:** Scaffold + Round 1 seeds only. The count is **provisional** until the fetch and screening rounds
below complete. Do not cite a final number yet.

**IDs:** `R5-###` (screened / used). Wishlist items use `R5W-###` (fetch-only, local).

## Snippets (paper-ready prose)

### R5-S1 — the scoped, factual version of the Introduction's "bottleneck" sentence

> Bulk purification of a virus — and the physical characterization that requires it — was, before cell
> culture, a capability confined to the agents that could be grown to high titer outside animal tissue:
> plant viruses such as tobacco mosaic virus (crystallized 1935) and bacteriophages. For the viruses of
> humans and animals, no general route of that kind existed; the first animal virus to be crystallized,
> poliovirus, was crystallized only in 1955 from tissue-culture-grown material. Cell culture therefore
> both supplied the material and, through the cytopathic-effect readout, credentialed the culture from
> which that material was drawn.

### R5-S2 — what a referee cannot rebut (process-critical, not "virology is false")

> These dates are a history of *methods capability*: absence of a pre-1949 bulk-purification route for
> animal viruses is a documented fact about the live-host bottleneck, not a claim that animal viruses were
> rare or unimportant before cell culture.
