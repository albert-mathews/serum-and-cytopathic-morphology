## Prediction

If culture CPE supernatant is not specific to viral presence, and serology tests are designed
with that supernatant as their reference (oracle), then the published record should contain cases
where **serology tests read positive on cell-culture reagents of the kind found in that
supernatant** — without requiring a virus to explain the signal.

*(Short form: serology tuned on CPE supernatant will react to culture-reagent material, not only
to a virus.)*

## Snippets (paper-ready prose)

**P4-S1 — there is no "perfect mock" (a matched uninfected culture cannot establish epitope identity).**
A matched uninfected culture is not a "no-virus" control in the sense the conclusion needs, because
there is no process that yields a pure viral-particle preparation to subtract. The infected and
uninfected cultures therefore differ by far more than the presence or absence of virus: the infected
culture is a degenerating monolayer whose cell debris, released host proteins, membrane vesicles, and
altered medium have no counterpart in the healthy mock. A positive signal on the infected culture that
is absent from the mock consequently shows only that the signal segregates with the infected state —
not that the reacting molecule is a viral epitope.

**P4-S2 — the recombinant antigen retraces, not escapes, the culture anchor.**
A recombinant antigen leaves the culture only at the production step, not at the point where it is
defined. Because its reading frame is in practice cloned from a reference strain that was itself
declared a culture+CPE Isolate — and whose assembly from a mixed culture product is, per P3, far from
error-free — the recombinant protein is the culture-direct antigen with a translation step interposed,
not an independent measurement of it.
