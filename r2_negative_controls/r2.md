# R2 — Isolation-stage negative controls (research stream)

**Folder:** `r2_negative_controls/`  
**Uses PDFs from:** `r1_isolation_standards_and_practice/refs/` (do not re-copy).  
**Goal:** Characterize the **incidence and quality** of negative controls in Isolation-practice reference publications (tiers A–D).

**Why the R1 corpus is the right frame for control evidence.** We trust the R1 corpus for reasonable
evidence of negative-control reporting because that corpus was selected *precisely because* those papers
were more thorough in reporting the methodological details of their cultures (numeric pre- and
post-inoculation serum stated). We therefore reasonably expect them to be at or near the **best** at
reporting negative controls. Read accordingly: the tier counts here are best treated as an *upper bound* on
control-reporting quality in the wider Isolation literature — a representative frame that is, if anything,
favorable to reporting, not a random sample.

**Concordance rule — R2 follows R1, always.** R2 does **not** maintain its own corpus. The scoring universe
is R1's category-1 set (`r1_isolation_standards_and_practice/isolation-refs.csv`, the reviewed single source of
truth): 181 unique Isolation-practice papers, of which R1 classifies **125 as class 1** (CPE read as virus
detection/Isolate) and **56 as class 4** (2I1D serum pattern with a different primary endpoint). R2 scores the
category-1 papers on their Isolation/infection culture arm regardless of class, and records `class` as an R1
fact. Category-2 items (protocols, `PR###`) and category-3 items (methods-reviews/teaching/theses, `MR###`) are
**out of R2 scope** — they prescribe or teach practice but do not report a run, so they cannot bear on the
incidence of Isolation-stage controls in reported practice. When R1 re-codes, re-classes, or moves a reference,
R2's `r2_universe`/`r1_status`/`r1_current_id` columns follow (recomputed by `wrk/r1_r2_align.py`). Any
disagreement between an R2 row and current R1 is corrected **on the R2 side**, with quotes from R1 records, R2
records, and the source PDFs (see `wrk/r1_r2_reconciliation_2026-11-19.md`).

## Snippets (paper-ready prose)

**Purpose:** Carefully worded sentences/paragraphs for later drafting. Prefer these over improvised strong language.  
**Rules:** Report what the corpus shows; ambiguity → worse/more conservative tier. Claims are limited to control quality as documented in the Isolation corpus.

### R2-S1 — What Tier A means for the paper

> A rigorous Isolation-stage negative control is a culture treated like the Isolation condition—including **matched maintenance/serum enrichment**—but **without** the viral inoculum. Controls that omit that match, or that exist only at a different stage, cannot audit whether CPE under Isolation conditions is virus-specific.

### R2-S2 — Scope of the scoring result

> We score Isolation-stage culture controls as reported in the paper corpus (tiers A–D). Absence or mismatch of controls is a **methods quality** finding about Isolation practice publications; it does not by itself prove that CPE is non-specific in every laboratory system.

### R2-S3 — Headline tier finding

> Of the 181 Isolation-practice papers in the R1 category-1 corpus, 103 (56.9%) described no culture-level negative control for the Isolation culture work (Tier D); 20 (11.0%) used a control only at a different stage or with an explicit mismatch (Tier C); 44 (24.3%) named an Isolation-stage control whose conditions could not be verified (Tier B); and 14 (7.7%) documented an audit-able matched control (Tier A). Among the 14 matched controls, the paper's own account of the control's CPE outcome (`nc_cpe_code`, §7.5b) is: **5** stated the control remained normal / showed no CPE; **3** showed or noted the control but without a cleanly stated CPE outcome; **5** named a control but never described its CPE/morphology; and **1 (VI117)** reported the control showing the protocol's CPE descriptors (detachment, nonspecific degeneration) under low serum — morphology the protocols themselves flag as a virus-independent confound. A second paper, **VI111** (Tier B), reported control-tissue cultures developing the same CPE. "CPE observed in the control" is judged by the protocol morphology descriptor set (not by an etiologic claim). *(Source: `control-cultures-overview.csv`, `r2_universe=yes`.)*

### R2-S4 — Serum-match crux

> Of the 181 scored category-1 papers, 17 (9.4%) verified that the negative culture shared the same serum/enrichment trajectory as the test culture (M3 = yes); 41 (22.7%) named an Isolation-stage control without a verifiable serum match (M3 = unknown); 1 (0.6%) explicitly mismatched the serum regime (M3 = no); and 122 (67.4%) described no culture-level control at all (M3 = NA). A matched-maintenance mock is therefore the exception, not the rule, across the scored corpus.

## Negative-control CPE coding (`nc_cpe_code`) — R3 cross-reference

**For R3 (image labeling / CPE morphology).** Each scored paper carries a `nc_cpe_code` (legend + protocol
definition in `control-cultures.md` §7.5b) recording whether the Isolation-stage no-virus control **showed CPE**,
plus free-text `nc_cpe_free` / verbatim `nc_cpe_quote` columns. Codes: **1** = control's reported appearance
includes any protocol CPE descriptor; **2** = control normal/no CPE; **3** = morphology noted but ambiguous;
**4** = control present, morphology not described; blank = no Isolation-stage control. "CPE" here = the
institutional-protocol morphology descriptor set (CLSI M41 Table 7 + ASM): rounded, ballooned/enlarged, refractile,
syncytia, vacuolation, detached, granularity, cytoplasmic strands, nonspecific degeneration — with the protocol's
per-virus development times (~1–10 days; CMV "may take 2–3 weeks or longer"). **No temporal cut-off excludes
morphology from being CPE.**

**Code-1 (virus-independent CPE) instances — the ones R3 will cite:**

| ID | Tier | Reported descriptors | Attribution in paper |
|----|------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **VI117** | A | detachment, nonspecific degeneration, abnormal gross morphology (rounding), over days–weeks in L-15 + 1–2% FCS, no virus ({Deig, Rie & Watkins 1970}) | spontaneous storage/aging, not virus — "Comparable uninfected cultures were maintained as controls." |
| **VI111** | B | rounded/refractile cells (HeLa); narrowed cells, irregular surface, intercellular gaps (MRC5) — after a longer delay; a blind study found the CPE **more often** in control-subject than Crohn's material ({Cytopathic effects in cultures inoculated with material from Crohn's disease}) | control-subject (non-disease) tissue — "…control tissue cultures remained healthy for longer but similar changes occurred in them eventually." |

Distribution by tier (181 papers): A = 1 code-1, 5 code-2, 3 code-3, 5 code-4; B = 1 code-1, 17 code-2, 6 code-3, 20 code-4; C = 1 code-3 (VI85, age-related changes) + 19 no-control; D = 103 no-control. *(Built by `wrk/nc_cpe_assignments.py` → `wrk/backfill_nc_cpe.py`.)*

**Additional virus-independent CPE/cytotoxin confounds (control stayed normal, but the paper documents non-viral CPE in the same system — relevant to R3 and the confound thesis, coded 2/3 with the note in `nc_cpe_free`):**

| ID | Tier | code | Non-viral CPE documented |
|----|------|------|--------------------------|
| **VI116** | B | 3 | C. difficile **toxin** produces "a cytopathic effect … similar to a viral cytopathic effect" (characteristic rounding + elongated cytoplasmic processes) in routine Isolation cultures (Schmidt, Ho & Dondero 1980). |
| **VI74** | B | 2 | **Serum (complement)** caused "similar cytopathic changes" to DENV-2, while the untreated negative control stayed normal. |
| **VI85** | C | 3 | Control cells showed "minimal **age-related** morphological changes attributable to prolonged culture duration," set against infected-cell detachment. |
