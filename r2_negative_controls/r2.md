# R2 — Isolation-stage negative controls (research stream)

**Folder:** `r2_negative_controls/`  
**Uses PDFs from:** `r1_isolation_standards_and_practice/refs/` (do not re-copy).  
**Goal:** Characterize the **incidence and quality** of negative controls in Isolation-practice reference publications (tiers A–D).

**Why the R1 corpus is the right frame for control evidence.** We trust the R1 corpus for reasonable
evidence of negative-control reporting because that corpus was selected *precisely because* those papers
were more thorough in reporting the methodological details of their cultures (numeric pre- and
post-inoculation serum stated). We therefore reasonably expect them to be at or near the **best** at
reporting negative controls. Read accordingly: the tier counts here are best treated as an *upper bound* on
control-reporting quality in the wider Isolation literature — a representative frame that is, if anything,
favorable to reporting, not a random sample.

## Snippets (paper-ready prose)

**Purpose:** Carefully worded sentences/paragraphs for later drafting. Prefer these over improvised strong language.  
**Rules:** Report what the corpus shows; ambiguity → worse/more conservative tier. Claims are limited to control quality as documented in the Isolation corpus.

### R2-S1 — What Tier A means for the paper

> A rigorous Isolation-stage negative control is a culture treated like the Isolation condition—including **matched maintenance/serum enrichment**—but **without** the viral inoculum. Controls that omit that match, or that exist only at a different stage, cannot audit whether CPE under Isolation conditions is virus-specific.

### R2-S2 — Scope of the scoring result

> We score Isolation-stage culture controls as reported in the paper corpus (tiers A–D). Absence or mismatch of controls is a **methods quality** finding about Isolation practice publications; it does not by itself prove that CPE is non-specific in every laboratory system.

### R2-S3 — Headline tier finding

> Of the 173 Isolation papers scored, 107 (61.8%) described no culture-level negative control for the Isolation culture work (Tier D); 14 (8.1%) used a control only at a different stage or with an explicit mismatch (Tier C); 40 (23.1%) named an Isolation-stage control whose conditions could not be verified (Tier B); and 12 (6.9%) documented an audit-able matched control (Tier A). None of the 12 Tier A controls was reported to show CPE. *(Source: `control-cultures-overview.csv`.)*

### R2-S4 — Serum-match crux

> Of the 173 papers, 13 (7.5%) verified that the negative culture shared the same serum/enrichment trajectory as the test culture (M3 = yes); 39 (22.5%) named an Isolation-stage control without a verifiable serum match (M3 = unknown); 1 (0.6%) explicitly mismatched the serum regime (M3 = no); and 120 (69.4%) described no culture-level control at all (M3 = NA). A matched-maintenance mock is therefore the exception, not the rule, across the scored corpus.
