# R6 — extract (R6-M5 primary backing: R6-022…R6-026)

All five fetched PDFs confirmed against the wishlist rows (title/authors/ID match), read in full, and `[VERIFIED
src]`. R6-M5 previously rested on two reviews (Riegman 2019 R6-020; Cheng & Ferrell 2018 R6-021); these five
underlying primaries now ground it directly. Two spatial scales are distinguished: **intracellular** (a discrete
focus → wave-relay through the cell's own mitochondria) and **intercellular** (dying cell → neighbouring cell →
tissue-scale front). Both are propagative, self-sustaining, and — for the ferroptosis branch — triggered by the
same serum/nutrient→cystine/GSH depletion that R6-M1/M2 supply.

## R6-022 — Garcia-Perez et al. 2012 (PNAS) — ROS-relayed mitochondrial permeabilisation wave (intracellular)

> "Bid-induced permeabilization and cytochrome c release regularly demonstrate a wave-like pattern, propagating
> through a cell at a constant velocity without dissipation." (abstract)

> "the cyto c–GFP release started at discrete sites and propagated as a wave over several-hundred-micrometer
> distances at full strength in a few minutes" (velocity H9c2 0.19 ± 0.01 μm/s; primary human cardiac muscle 0.31
> ± 0.05 μm/s).

> "reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavengers suppressed the coordination of cytochrome c release and also inhibited
> Bid-induced cell death, whereas both superoxide and hydrogen peroxide sensitized mitochondria to Bid-induced
> permeabilization."

⇒ "Bid engages a ROS-dependent, local **intermitochondrial** potentiation mechanism that amplifies the apoptotic
signal as a wave." **Scope note:** this wave is *intracellular* (mitochondrion-to-mitochondrion within one cell),
not cell-to-cell — a correction to the Round-2 wishlist's "intercellular" tag. It is one of the primaries Cheng &
Ferrell 2018 cite for "apoptosis initiates at a discrete focus and spreads."

## R6-025 — Pacher & Hajnóczky 2001 (EMBO J) — mitochondrial waves propagate the apoptotic signal (intracellular)

> "Generation of mitochondrial signals is believed to be important in the commitment to apoptosis … Ca2+ spikes
> initiate depolarization of mitochondria in discrete sub-cellular regions, and these mitochondria initiate slow
> waves of depolarization and Ca2+ release propagating through the cell."

> "Cellular signals originating from discrete cellular domains often give rise to traveling messenger waves that
> relay the signal at full strength to remote intracellular targets or even to other cells."

> "Expansion of the local excitation by mitochondrial waves propagating through the whole cell can be especially
> important in activation of the apoptotic machinery in large cells."

⇒ The seminal statement that a single focus can seed a **self-regenerating wave** that synchronises the apoptotic
program across the whole cell (Bcl-xL-suppressible; PTP-dependent; cyto c release → caspase → nuclear apoptosis).
Founded in cardiac myotubes (H9c2). The primary that both Cheng & Ferrell 2018 and Garcia-Perez 2012 build on.

## R6-023 — Linkermann et al. 2014 (PNAS) — ferroptosis synchronises cell-to-cell tubular death (intercellular)

> "iron-dependent ferroptosis directly causes synchronized necrosis of renal tubules" (abstract).

> "The dynamics of the tubular necrosis suggest a **direct cell-to-cell communication to deliver the deadly
> signal**" — "synchronized tubular cell death very much resembles the appearance of casts found in urine
> sediments of patients with acute kidney injury."

> Mechanics stated in-passing: ferroptosis "is an iron-dependent necrotic type of cell death that occurs due to
> lipid peroxide accumulation, which routinely is prevented by glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), a glutathione
> (GSH)-dependent enzyme, and therefore depends on the functionality of a glu/cys antiporter in the plasma
> membrane referred to as system Xc-minus."

⇒ The key intercellular primary at the epithelial/functional-unit scale: whole tubules die in synchrony by a
cell-to-cell ferroptotic signal — and its trigger is exactly the **cystine/GSH/GPX4** axis that a serum step-down
depletes. Cited by Kim 2016 (ref 22) and Tang 2026 (ref 16).

## R6-024 — Kim et al. 2016 (Nat Nanotechnol) — ferroptosis propagates cell-to-cell as a wave, unlike apoptosis

> "ferroptosis in this context was also observed to propagate from cell to cell in a wave-like manner (Fig. 4e,
> Supplementary Movie 1), **unlike that found for cells undergoing other types of death, such as apoptosis** …
> These findings suggested cell-cell communication of a death-inducing signal, similar to a recent report of
> ferroptosis occurring in renal tubules in response to treatment with erastin."

> Trigger was **nutrient deprivation + glutathione depletion**: death arose in amino-acid-deprived cells in
> combination with an iron-loading particle; rescued by ferrostatin-1/liproxstatin-1 (lipid-ROS scavengers), the
> iron chelator deferoxamine, and by glutathione repletion (GSH or its precursor N-acetylcysteine).

⇒ The direct primary showing the ferroptotic wave front travels **between cells** (not just within one), and that
the wave's substrate is iron-dependent lipid peroxidation with glutathione as the rate-limiting brake — i.e. the
M1 (serum → ↓cystine/GSH) → M5 (propagative ferroptotic wave) bridge, in a nutrient-deprivation model (MCF10A
epithelial, MEF, HT-1080, several carcinoma lines).

## R6-026 — Tang, Kang & Kroemer 2026 (Cell Res — Perspective) — ferroptotic propagation is multiscale

> "ferroptotic commitment can extend beyond the initiating cell, spreading to neighboring cells and, in some
> contexts, across tissue-scale distances. Multiple, non-mutually exclusive modes of contagion have now been
> described, including reactive oxygen species-triggered death waves, direct membrane contact-dependent transfer,
> and extracellular vesicle-mediated paracrine signaling."

> "In confluent cell monolayers, including epithelial models such as MCF10A mammary epithelial cells, ferroptotic
> death can propagate in wave-like spatiotemporal fronts through neighboring cells." And: in "highly confluent
> epithelial monolayers … cells undergo progressive swelling and death in a wave-like front that advances across
> the monolayer over time. This ordered progression, initiated from a defined focus of ferroptotic stress, strongly
> supports a propagative process rather than simultaneous co-injury."

> ROS trigger wave: "localized ferroptotic stress gives rise to self-sustaining oxidative fronts that travel across
> the culture over distances exceeding 5 mm at a nearly constant velocity of ~5.5 μm/min"; "the maintenance of an
> approximately constant wave velocity strongly supports the existence of a reaction-diffusion-like excitable
> system."

> Starvation lowers the propagation threshold: "under nutrient-depleted or amino acid starvation conditions,
> intracellular glutathione levels progressively decline, thereby lowering the execution threshold for GPX4
> inhibition-induced ferroptosis. This metabolic shift converts an initially heterogeneous and largely
> cell-autonomous response into uniform collective necrosis accompanied by extensive lysosome rupture and wave-like
> multicellular propagation. In this setting, glutathione depletion acts as a critical sensitizing factor that links
> intracellular redox-buffering capacity to tissue-level ferroptotic spread."

> The morphology vocabulary is ours: affected cells "form contiguous zones of degeneration, synchronized tubular
> epithelial loss, aligned myofiber injury, or expanding fronts of tissue damage."

**Devil's-advocate caveat (keep).** Tang et al. insist "spatial clustering of dead cells, even when highly ordered,
does not by itself demonstrate that one dying cell actively drives the death of another. Cells occupying the same
microenvironment may simply be exposed to a shared upstream insult." Their three operational criteria for *true*
propagation — (i) a spatially defined initiation site, (ii) mechanistic continuity, (iii) ferroptosis specificity
— are the same discipline R6 applies; and they flag that "most mechanistic dissection has thus far been performed
in highly confluent two-dimensional cell culture systems." This is the honest cross-check for the CRO record
(sub-confluent ~80%): M5's focal-clearing claim does not rest on the wave-front geometry alone, but on the
**serum-dose differential (2% vs 10%)** plus the deprivation→GSH→ferroptosis trigger, which R6-M1/M2 supply.
