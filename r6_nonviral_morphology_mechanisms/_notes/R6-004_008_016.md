# R6 — extract (Vero serum/nutrient primaries + plaque theory)

Verbatim extracts used to ground mechanisms R6-M1/M2 (nutrient/growth-factor withdrawal) and R6-M11 + framing-2
(diffusion-limited plaque). All `[VERIFIED src]` (full-text PDFs in `refs/`).

## R6-004 — Mendonça et al. 2009 (Vero nutrient-depletion morphology, EM + light)

> "Previously we have described the apoptosis occurrence when VERO cells are cultured under glutamine or
> galactose depletion. … The following changes were observed by light microscopy: brightness and progressive
> detachment of cells from monolayer, formation of cell surface in folds with multiple blebs and finally release
> of several membrane-bound apoptotic bodies. At ultra structural level, the apoptotic cells were characterized
> by condensed chromatin, intensive nuclear fragmentation, cytoplasmic organelle disruption, formation of
> apoptotic bodies and cell shrinkage." (abstract)

Body repeatedly documents "Vacuoles (v) are present", "increase of granularity", "granular endoplasmic
reticulum", and membrane "blebbing" through the apoptotic sequence.

**R6 map:** this is the Vero-specific warrant that nutrient depletion → the CLSI descriptors *granularity*,
*vacuolation*, *detachment*, and the CRO terms *bright/dying cells* via apoptosis — supporting R6-M2 (and, with
Engelhardt & Mao 1977 on serum-dependent G2 arrest, R6-M1).

## R6-016 — Quesney et al. 2001 (Vero, serum-containing vs serum-free bioreactor)

> "A higher death rate in a serum-free medium (vs. [serum-containing]) … cells die from apoptosis followed by
> secondary necrosis." (abstract / discussion; body)

> "Apoptosis, evidenced by annexin V-FITC/PI staining, could be detected in the population of suspension
> [detached] cells … the lower cell yield in SfB … results from a higher cell death rate rather than from a
> lower cell division."

> "LDH release in the supernatant increased much earlier in SfB than in ScA (one vs. five days)."

**R6 map:** Vero-specific warrant that lowering the serum/nutrient supply raises the death rate by **apoptosis →
secondary necrosis**, with earlier LDH release — the same "dying cells + detachment" signature reported in the
low-serum CRO arm (OB024/OB025). Supports R6-M1/M2 and the necrotic secondary phase.

## R6-008 — Abedon & Yin 2009 (phage plaque theory)

> "…plaque formation failure is not necessarily equivalent to virion inviability (Note 1)." (abstract + body §Note 1)

> "Anything that slows phage diffusion can impede [plaque growth] … agar density, the slower phage diffusion."

> Plaque growth is governed by a "hindered diffusion constant D<sub>eff</sub>" and yields a prediction of final
> plaque radius — i.e., plaque size is a function of **virion/host motility and gel density**, and plaque size is
> also coupled to "rates of nutrient and waste-product diffusion into and [out of]" the growing focus.

**R6 map:** warrant for **framing-2** — the focal clearing (plaque) is a *diffusion-limited spatial growth
pattern* whose size/shape is set by diffusivity and resource transport, and whose *failure to appear* is not
proof of particle inviability. This is the formal ground for "one plaque = one particle" being a co-variance
assumption rather than a pattern-uniqueness proof: the same geometry arises for any growing focused lesion under
a diffusion constraint.
