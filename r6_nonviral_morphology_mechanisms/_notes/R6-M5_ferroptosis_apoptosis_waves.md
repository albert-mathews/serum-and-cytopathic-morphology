## R6-020 — Riegman, Bradbury & Overholtzer 2019 (Trends Cancer)

> "a regulated mechanism of necrosis called ferroptosis has the unusual ability to spread between cells in a
> wave-like manner, thereby eliminating entire cell populations." (abstract)

> "The demise of an individual cell can be a completely autonomous event… but death can also affect neighboring
> cell viability… through what is called a bystander effect… the death of individual cells can trigger the
> spreading of trans factors… Deaths in this context often occur in a successive manner, with an expanding,
> wave-like appearance."

> "Ferroptosis has the ability to spread through cell populations and may be an intrinsically propagative
> mechanism."

> "We have shown previously that cell populations experiencing long-term stress (e.g. nutrient starvation)
> exhibit mixed cell responses involving different types of cell death, including apoptosis, necrosis and
> entosis…"  — nutrient starvation → **mixed** cell death (not one clean program).

> "In a recent report, the detachment of breast epithelial cells from extracellular matrix… was observed to lead
> to ferroptotic death."  — links M6 (anoikis/detachment) to the same ferroptosis pathway.

**Ferroptosis trigger (the M1→M5 bridge), in their words:** erastin "inhibits the cystine/glutamate antiporter
system xc⁻ … depletes intracellular cysteine and thereby inhibits generation of the major antioxidant
glutathione. The resulting loss of antioxidant capacity renders cells susceptible to the detrimental effects of
reactive oxygen species (ROS)… In the presence of intracellular iron these lipid ROS can set off a chain
reaction causing the spread of lipid peroxides throughout cell membranes."

## R6-021 — Cheng & Ferrell 2018 (Science; PMC6263143)

> "apoptosis spreads through trigger waves in both extracts and intact cells… allowing diverse all-or-none
> decisions to propagate over large distances without decreasing in speed or amplitude." (abstract)

> "Trigger waves are propagating fronts of chemical activity that maintain a constant speed and amplitude over
> large distances. They can arise when bistable biochemical reactions are subject to diffusion, or… when
> something akin to bistability… is combined with a spatial coupling mechanism (e.g. diffusion or cell-cell
> communication)."

> "Imaging studies on mammalian cell lines… cardiac myotubes… and syncytial human trophoblasts have shown that
> apoptosis typically initiates at a single discrete focus, or a small number of discrete foci, and then spreads
> rapidly…" (cites Garcia-Perez 2012 ROS-mediated intercellular wave; Pacher & Hajnóczky 2001.)

## R6 reading (scoped, per AX011)

The two facts R6-M5 needs are now sourced: (i) **death is propagative** — a single dying cell can kill its
neighbours in an expanding front (ferroptosis wave; "apoptosis-induced apoptosis"/bystander effect), giving a
focal, contiguous, countable zone of death whose size/shape is set by diffusivity and resource transport; and
(ii) **the trigger is the serum/nutrient step itself** — serum step-down removes cystine (system xc⁻) and
lipophilic antioxidants, lowering glutathione, so lipid peroxidation → ferroptosis is the expected mode, which
then *spreads*. This ties M1 (serum withdrawal) → M5 (propagative death) into a single mechanism chain, and
explains the focal, "plaque-like" clearing that need not be viral. Cheng & Ferrell's "apoptosis initiates at a
single discrete focus and spreads" supplies the same for the apoptotic branch (matched by Quesney 2001's
Vero "apoptosis → secondary necrosis").
