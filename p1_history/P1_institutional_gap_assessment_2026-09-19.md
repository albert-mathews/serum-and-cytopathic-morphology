# P1 institutional / historical gap assessment — 2026-09-19

## Decision

**Enough: recommend NO further P1 institutional-history search.** The existing P1 spine, together with the institutional end-markers already held in R1, is sufficient to support the limited claim that a mid-century growth-versus-maintenance distinction was later codified in diagnostic manuals and persists in institutional recipes. P1 should remain closed as a contextual Discussion thread. Modern Isolation papers remain in R1.

This is not a claim that every virus, cell line, or laboratory used the same recipe. The records instead support a narrower and safer statement: the operational distinction is recurrent, historically documented, and later standardized with commonly lower serum in the post-confluence/inoculation phase.

## What the existing record can support

1. **Dual growth/maintenance practice was real by the mid-century period.** The 1960 Sheffield record and the 1969 Schmidt/Lennette diagnostic-manual chapter make this more than a modern-web-guide observation.
2. **Serum reduction was operationally tied to virus work and CPE readability.** The P1 primary spine (Melnick, Scherer, Youngner, Baron & Low, Boyle) supplies the historical motives and recipe variation; the manuals show later institutional codification.
3. **The institutional rationale is heterogeneous.** Existing records mention inhibitors/antibodies, attachment, overgrowth control, or only a procedural instruction. P1 should not imply one universal rationale.
4. **The control-design gap remains a separate empirical point.** Manuals and historical protocols document how cultures were run; they do not supply a foundational matched, uninfected, serum-only experiment demonstrating that CPE is virus-specific under the same Isolation conditions. The existing P1 wording (“we did not find…”) is appropriately bounded and should remain a search result, not an absolute historical negative.
5. **The modern 10% → approximately 2% recipe is a codified endpoint, not proof that all earlier practice was uniform.** State this explicitly to avoid overclaiming from institutional examples.
